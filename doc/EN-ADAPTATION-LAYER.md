# 2. Core Adaptation Principle

English is not a literal translation of Polish.

English must be:

- Procedural
- Institutional
- Boundary-aware
- Legally neutral
- Clear for international property owners

If literal translation reduces clarity:
→ Adapt structurally.

Structure prevails over linguistic symmetry.

# 4A. Usage Pathway Terminology (Binding)

Public English pathway labels:

- Private Absence
- Active Guest Use
- Mixed / Undetermined Use

Allowed contextual variants:

- Private Use (only inside estimator controls where space is constrained)
- Mixed / Not Decided (homepage card label only)

Not allowed:

- Empty apartment customer
- Rental customer
- Undecided customer
- Holiday-home care
- Rental management

Rules:

- Pathway labels classify property use, not customer personality.
- Do not imply different packages or different SLA.
- Do not soften into hospitality language.

---

# 4B. Whole-Site Coherence Terms (Binding)

- `property use` is the general classification term. Preserve the approved public pathway labels and protected pathway slugs.
- `qualification` means the pre-service fit/scope review. `inquiry` means the form or message submitted to Sentinel; they are not synonyms.
- The canonical primary route-to-Contact CTA is `Start qualification`. Accepted special-purpose CTA exceptions remain valid for genuinely different actions.
- Exact artifact terms are `visit report`, `operational record`, `decision request`, and `action summary`.
- When several artifacts are meant, use ordinary collective language such as `documentation` or `documents and records`. Do not turn `owner record` into a general branded category.
- Use the full term when introducing or controlling meaning. After context is established, `Selected property use`, `Change selection`, and `After selection` are approved contextual labels.
- Avoid mechanical repetition of `agreed process`, `operating scope`, and similar framework nouns. Prefer concrete English such as check scope, access rules, visit rhythm, response timing, next steps, or action limits when those are the facts meant.
- Natural English must not imply that Sentinel owns the whole situation, accepts unlimited responsibility, or changes package, authority, SLA, or liability meaning.

# 6. Parameter Integrity Rule

Numeric precision must be preserved.

Example:

PL:
„Reakcja w ciągu 24h.”

EN:
“Response within 24h.”

Not:
“Fast response.”

Limits must remain measurable.

---

# 7. PL / EN Synchronization Rule (Operational)

Cursor must:

- Apply structural updates in both languages.
- Preserve parameter clarity in both.
- Avoid partial language updates.
- Avoid postponing EN adaptation.

If refinement is imperfect:
→ Owner review will correct.
Structure must remain aligned.

Parity prevails over elegance.

---

# 8. FAQ Adaptation Rule

In English:

- Use shorter declarative sentences.
- Avoid modal softness (may, might, could) unless legally required.
- Prefer defined boundaries.

Example:

PL:
„Sentinel nie świadczy usług prawnych.”

EN:
“Sentinel does not provide legal advice.”

Not:
“Sentinel may not provide legal services.”

---

# 9. Tone Calibration for Target Owner Segment (EN)

English must read as:

- Structured
- Professional
- Non-sales
- Internationally neutral

Avoid regionally casual phrasing.
Avoid idiomatic expressions.

English may be clear and direct without becoming casual sales copy. Prefer institutional clarity over idioms.
